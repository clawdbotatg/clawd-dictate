## Decisions (don't relitigate)

1. **Deepgram, not Wispr Flow / Whisper apps.** They batch-transcribe; he
   wants live text. Wispr Flow was installed, hated ("predatory UI"), killed.
2. **Rules, not just keyterms.** A keyterm nudges; two common words that sound
   the same ("on chain") still win. Rules rewrite every time.
3. **Mac: a real .app bundle.** Permissions are granted to "clawd-dictate",
   not `python`, and survive `git pull` (launcher binary never changes).
   Rebuilding the launcher resets the grants — avoid.
4. **Phone: mic HELD open between dictations, socket only during one.** iOS
   refuses to start a mic from the background; releasing it meant a hop into
   the app on every keyboard use (tried, rejected: "I want good UX"). The
   orange dot between dictations = hardware open; the Deepgram socket does not
   exist then. Austin checked Deepgram's usage log: nothing between
   dictations. The guarantee is spelled out atop `ios/App/Session.swift`.
5. **Wake path on iOS 18+:** only `EnvironmentValues().openURL` and a SwiftUI
   `Link` open the app from a keyboard. Responder-chain `openURL:` and
   `extensionContext.open` are dead. The keyboard ADOPTS the dictation the
   hop started instead of starting another (that restart was the flapping).
6. **Nothing personal in the repo.** No keys, no credentials, and none of
   Austin's URLs (relay, calendar links). `~/.config/clawd-dictate/env`
   (HARNESS_URL, CAL_URL, SLOP_URL, DEEPGRAM_API_KEY, DOCS_CREDENTIAL) →
   `ios/gen-secrets.sh` → gitignored `Secrets.swift`. History was squashed
   and the GitHub repo deleted + recreated on 09-15 to purge two commits that
   had the URLs. Grep for your personal domains (the ones in `~/.config/clawd-dictate/env`) before every commit.
